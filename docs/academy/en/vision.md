# Vision and learning objectives

**Language:** English · [Italiano](../it/vision.md)

## Vision

VirtualShip Academy teaches learners to design, justify and execute a digital oceanographic expedition. The learner takes the role of an onboard scientific lead: they must read the chart, choose stations and instruments, respect operational constraints, produce a reproducible configuration and interpret the results.

The product combines three forms of literacy:

1. **Oceanographic** — seafloor, water column, instruments, sampling and data limitations.
2. **Computational** — structured configurations, revisions, validation, terminal and pipelines.
3. **Operational** — route, timing, safety, vessel preparation, errors and recovery.

## Audience

- bachelor's students encountering oceanographic planning for the first time;
- master's students comparing sampling designs and trade-offs;
- thesis students using VirtualShip and OceanParcels as reproducible research tools;
- educators who need to observe the decision process, not only the final answer;
- researchers and developers who need a readable bridge to upstream VirtualShip.

## Cross-level learning outcomes

After completing the full progression, a learner should be able to:

- formulate an oceanographic question that can be translated into a sampling plan;
- select stations compatible with bathymetry, instruments and safety clearances;
- justify sensor and platform choices;
- distinguish observation, simulation, derivation and demonstration data;
- read and modify a valid `expedition.yaml` configuration;
- recognise the relationship between GUI, files, commands and results;
- document provenance, assumptions, limitations and revisions;
- recover from errors without losing decision traceability;
- produce a scientific artefact that another person can reproduce.

## What makes the Academy a game

The game dimension comes from role, objectives, constraints, feedback and consequences rather than decorative points. Every mission contains:

- an explicit scientific assignment;
- available resources and information;
- reversible and irreversible decisions;
- safety and validation checks;
- an observable operational sequence;
- a debrief connecting decision, evidence and consequence.

## Non-goals

The Academy must not:

- replace a certified nautical chart or real navigation tool;
- present a pedagogical visualisation as an in-situ measurement;
- expose Copernicus credentials, an operating-system shell or HPC infrastructure to learners;
- reduce success to speed, clicks or memorised procedures;
- hide errors behind unexplained automatic corrections;
- promise live computation that the vertical slice does not perform.

## Tone

The tone is academic, operational and accessible. Technical terms are introduced when they become useful. Difficulty grows through autonomy and the quality of reasoning, not through deliberately hostile interfaces.
